SOC Alert Triage Lab

Turn a sequence of sign-ins into a reviewable incident timeline.

Synthetic lab result

3 failed sign-ins, then 1 success

Problem and audience

Analysts need the event sequence and a defensible reason to escalate. The synthetic case starts with three failed sign-ins at 09:00–09:02 UTC and a success at 09:04 for the same account and source.

Implementation

The Python implementation reads CSV, sorts by timestamp, and groups by account and source. It emits the matching failure and success timestamps as JSON. A success clears that group’s failure history.

Python · CSV · unittest

Validation and results

Five tests cover the fixture, the exact window boundary, expired failures, separate sources, and the reset after a success.

Limitations and lessons

A match warrants review. MFA, device context, and activity after the login are needed to decide whether to escalate.

How I’d triage the alert

  1. Confirm that the failures and success refer to the same account and source. Check the timestamps and direction of the sequence.
  2. Look for an expected password reset or a known device. Check the MFA outcome and whether the login matches the account’s normal activity.
  3. Review what the account did after login. Escalate if that activity or the device and MFA records add evidence of unauthorized access.

In this exercise the alert remains open because the log records the sequence but lacks that supporting context.
